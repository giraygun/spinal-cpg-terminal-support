# Draft captions for Figures 4-8

## Figure 4. Intact-network phase stability across descending drive and mechanical load.

Heat maps show exact values in all nine no-pulse descending-drive-by-load cells: (A) L-R and (B) F-E absolute phase error, and (C) L-R and (D) F-E slip fractions with raw slip/cycle counts retained in the panel data. All nine conditions remained rhythmic. The 45-degree criterion is the frozen slip threshold. All values are conditional on one frozen stochastic network realization. Medians and ranges summarize the prespecified design grid and are not sampling-uncertainty estimates.

## Figure 5. Pulse-evoked phase change and full-follow-up recovery in the intact network.

Exact post-pulse minus direction-matched sham differences are shown for (A) L-R and (B) F-E phase error across all descending-drive-by-load cells; horizontal bars are design-grid medians. (C) Recovery endpoint eligibility and event status are reported separately from phase effects. (D) Times are shown only for observed recovery events; full-follow-up censor fields remain in the panel data. Marker shape denotes the low, medium, or high descending-drive command and color denotes load. All values are conditional on one frozen stochastic network realization. Medians and ranges summarize the prespecified design grid and are not sampling-uncertainty estimates.

## Figure 6. Circuit interventions and recruitment across descending-drive commands.

Design-grid medians of exact context-matched phase effects are shown for (A) ten single interventions and (B) six paired interventions. (C) Paired-intervention nonadditivity is pair - single 1 - single 2 + intact; positive values indicate a larger phase burden than the additive expectation. (D) Exact intact-network firing rates show recruitment across descending-drive and pulse contexts. The command level is an experimental input and is not treated as measured locomotor speed or assumed to yield monotonic network frequency. Failure and recovery counts are reported separately in Table R3. All values are conditional on one frozen stochastic network realization. Medians and ranges summarize the prespecified design grid and are not sampling-uncertainty estimates.

## Figure 7. Presynaptic route effects and class-by-route dependencies.

(A) Route impairment minus exact matched intact phase effects and (B) route-local changes in phenomenological terminal support, readily releasable pool, and replenishment resource. (C-H) Full 10-by-10 class-by-route nonadditivity matrices for L-R and F-E phase error under no-pulse, excitatory, and inhibitory contexts. Outlined cells are diagonal class-route combinations; gray cells are undefined because all four rhythmic arms were not available. Terminal-state measures explain model mechanism but are not evidence for microtubule biology. All values are conditional on one frozen stochastic network realization. Medians and ranges summarize the prespecified design grid and are not sampling-uncertainty estimates.

## Figure 8. Composite-stress dynamics and terminal-support specificity.

Epoch trajectories for (A) L-R and (B) F-E phase error show route-grid medians and full ranges; the shared no-challenge intact arm is a single trajectory. Shading marks the locked baseline, stress pre-challenge, stress-plus-challenge, and recovery windows; epochs 1 and 19 are display-only transitions. Exact route-level challenge-by-impairment effects are shown for (C) L-R and (D) F-E phase error. (E) Dynamic terminal support is compared with static-matched, time-yoked, and spatially shuffled controls within each KCa mode. (F) Exact difference-in-differences values show KCa modulation of time- and location-specific contrasts. All values are conditional on one frozen stochastic network realization. Medians and ranges summarize the prespecified design grid and are not sampling-uncertainty estimates.
